The four lessons

1 · There's more than one method (slide 6)

  • Standard, Part III — rebuilds the house circuit by circuit. The calc for a house being built.
  • 220.82 — whole-house shortcut. Needs 100 A service or bigger.
  • 220.83 — existing house, adding a load. That's you.
  • 220.87 — don't estimate. Measure.
"A lot of guys quoting you have never run any of these. They counted your breakers. That's not one of the four."say it without sneering — you're explaining, not attacking

2 · 220.83 (slide 7)

Read the quote out loud, slowly. Then translate: "Existing service. Additional load. That's a house getting an EV charger."

Mechanics: first 8 kVA at 100%, everything after that at 40%.

(A) vs (B) if asked: (B) is when you're also installing new AC or electric heat. It's about what you're putting in, not what the house already has. A charger alone is (A).

Say the edition out loud: "That's the 2017 wording — we're on 2023 in Kentucky, these sections read the same, but I'm quoting the copy I can legally hand you." That sentence buys you more credibility than anything else in the talk.

3 · 220.87 — the one nobody teaches (slide 8)

"Take the highest your house has actually pulled. Multiply by one-two-five. Add the charger. If that's under your service rating, you're done — and you never estimated anything."

How they get it: a year of demand data from the utility, or 30 days on a recording meter with the house occupied and the heat or AC running. The code defines the peak as the highest 15-minute average — the exact interval utilities already report.

4 · Your lesson — do it live (slide 11)

Live demos — how they don't go sideways

Slide 10 — the panel

Ask it this way: "Who's got a house they know the numbers on? Roughly how big, and are you 100 or 200 amp?"

  • Name the method while the cite's on screen: "This gauge runs 220.82 — the stricter estimate. Same house: this one reads 127 where 220.83 reads 113. Fit under the strict one and there's no argument."
  • Don't know their service size → "Look at the main breaker — the big one at the top. It's got a number on it." Default to 200.
  • Nobody volunteers → use your own house. Never stand there waiting.
  • Comes back red → that's a gift: "That's the one in five. And you already know two ways out — charge slower, or pull your real demand off the meter."

If you blank

  1. "Let me say that a different way." — sounds deliberate, resets you.
  2. "The short version is—" then the spine line.
  3. "Here, let me just show you." — jump to slide 10 or 11 and run it live.
Blanking looks like thinking. Don't apologize for a pause. That's what the water is for.

The landmine — someone already paid

Get ahead of it on slide 13. Absolve first:

"And if you already upgraded — that's not on you. You did what anybody does, you trusted the guy with the license. You're in good shape now: room for a second car, a heat pump, whatever. I just want the next person to get asked the question."
Don't give that person the charge-slower lesson. You'd be telling them there were two ways they didn't need to spend. Save it for someone who comes back red.
