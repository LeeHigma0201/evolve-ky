# A/B TESTS — the four moments that decide the talk

**How we pick a winner:** not by which reads better — by **which one you can say out loud, cold, without looking.** A line you have to remember is a line you'll fumble. Say both, keep the one that comes out in your own mouth.

Record picks in [fumble-log.md](fumble-log.md).

---

## TEST 1 — THE COLD OPEN (first 15 seconds)

Highest-stakes 15 seconds of the day. The room decides whether to listen.

**A · The question opener**
> "Show of hands — who's been quoted for a panel upgrade to charge at home?
> *(hands)* Yeah. What'd they tell you, three grand? Four?
> Most of you didn't need it. That's what I want to talk about."

**B · The confession opener**
> "I'm an electrician, and I'm going to talk you out of buying something from an electrician.
> Four out of five of you don't need the panel upgrade you're about to be quoted for.
> Let me show you how to know which one you are."

**C · The cold-number opener**
> "Three to five thousand dollars.
> *(pause)* That's what it costs to upgrade a panel. And most of you are about to be told you need one.
> You probably don't."

> **Recommendation: A.** Hands in the air in the first 10 seconds means the room is participating instead of watching, and their own hands prove your point for you. **B is the backup** if the room feels small or shy — it gets a laugh and it's pure you.
> **C only if you're rattled** — it needs zero audience cooperation and works even if you're nervous. Keep it in your back pocket.

## TEST 2 — THE ANCHOR (slide 3, the $3–5k)

The money has to *hurt* before you take it back.

**A · Let it hang**
> "Three to five thousand dollars."
> **(count three full seconds. say nothing.)**
> "Most of you won't spend a dime of that."

**B · Make it personal**
> "Three to five thousand dollars. That's a set of tires, a vacation, most of a used motorcycle.
> And most of you won't spend a dime of it."

> **Recommendation: A.** The silence does more work than any comparison. Three seconds feels like forever on stage and like one second in the audience — **count it in your head.** B is fine but it's busier, and the extra words let them off the hook.

## TEST 3 — THE AHA (slide 7 — the comprehension unlock)

If they don't get this, the calculator is just numbers. This is the most important 30 seconds in the deck.

**A · The "everything at once" frame**
> "Here's the thing nobody explains. Your panel's sized like everybody's running the oven, the dryer, the AC and the water heater at the exact same time. You never do that. Your house is barely working most of the day — and your car charges at two in the morning, when literally all of it is off."

**B · The parking-garage frame**
> "Think of your panel like a parking garage. It's got a hundred spaces, and the quote assumes every space is full at rush hour. But your house is a garage that's half empty most of the day — and the EV parks at 2 AM when everybody else has gone home."

**C · The one-sentence version**
> "Your panel is barely working, and your car charges while you're asleep. That's it. That's the whole thing."

> **Recommendation: A, then C as the button.** A paints the picture with *their* appliances, then C stamps it so they can repeat it. Say A, pause, then say C slower.
> **Skip B.** Metaphors invite arguing about the metaphor — an engineer will start asking about garage capacity instead of listening.

## TEST 4 — THE ASK (slide 12 — turn the room into your marketing)

Your stated goal: word of mouth. This is the only slide that does it.

**A · The favor**
> "Somebody you know is about to get quoted four thousand dollars for something they don't need. Scan this and text it to them — right now, while you're sitting here. That's the whole favor."

**B · The named person**
> "Think of one person — one — who's talked about getting an EV. You've got somebody. Text them this link right now. When they don't spend four grand, that was you."

> **Recommendation: B.** "Think of one person" makes them actually retrieve a face from memory, and once they've got a face they're far more likely to send it. It also ends on **"that was you"** — you're handing them the credit, which is exactly what makes people share.
> **A is safer and shorter.** If you're at time, use A.

## THINGS TO DROP IF YOU'RE RUNNING LONG

In order — cut from the top:
1. **Slide 6** (why the quote) — the villain is implied by slide 5 anyway
2. **Slide 5** (who does need it) — but then you *must* say "and I'll tell you when you do need it" out loud on slide 4, or you lose the honesty
3. **Second demo house** on slide 8 — one house is enough

**Never cut:** 3 (the money), 7 (the aha), 8 (the live calc), 11 (the share line), 12 (the QR).
