# Q&A BANK — the room, simulated

Simulated Evolve KY audience: EV-club regulars (Leaf/Bolt/Model 3 owners), 2–4 people who just bought and haven't installed charging, at least one engineer who will test your math, likely one electrician or contractor, someone who already paid for an upgrade, someone in a condo, someone with solar.

**Answer format that works every time:** short answer first → one sentence of why → stop. Don't keep talking. The silence after a short answer reads as confidence.

---

## TIER 1 — you will almost certainly get these

### "So are you selling something?"
> **"I'm an electrician. The app's free to check. If it turns out you need work, I'd love the job — but four out of five of you won't, and I'd rather you know that than not."**

That's the whole answer. Don't get defensive, don't over-explain. Owning it is what makes it land.

### "Can you do my install?"
> **"Yeah. Grab me after and let's look at your panel."**

Don't quote a price in the room. **"Depends what I find — let me look first"** is the honest answer and it's also the right sales answer.

### "How much does the app cost?"
> **"Checking your panel is free. Go do it tonight."**

### "I already upgraded my panel. Did I waste my money?"
> **"No. You've got room for everything now — an extra car, a heat pump down the road, and it helps when you sell. I just want the next guy to get the choice you didn't get."**

⚠️ **Most important answer in this document.** Warm, fast, no hesitation. Then move on — don't dwell, it prolongs their embarrassment.

### "What if I'm in an apartment or condo?"
> **"Then this isn't your fight — yours is with the HOA or the property manager, and it's a different conversation. But send this to anyone you know with a house."**

Honest, quick, and turns a dead end into a share.

### "How accurate is it, really?"
> **"It's the same calculation I'd do by hand on a service call — the optional dwelling method. Same numbers, same answer. The app just does it from a photo instead of me with a clipboard."**

## TIER 2 — the engineer / the electrician in the room

### "Where does the 80% come from? That's not a code threshold for the whole service." ⚠️ REAL GOTCHA
He's right, and agreeing makes you look like the sharpest guy in the room:

> **"Good catch — it's not. The code pass/fail is calculated load versus your service rating, straight up. The 80% is my comfort line, because anything continuous gets sized at 125%, and the flip side of 125% is 80%. So I show three bands: under 80% you're comfortable, 80 to 100 it fits but it's tight, over 100 you've got a real problem. I'd rather be conservative on somebody's house."**

**This is your own documented open question — you already know the answer. Don't get rattled by it.**

### "You're not accounting for the EV as a continuous load."
> **"I am — the charger goes in at 100% of nameplate, no demand factor. It's the one thing in the calc that doesn't get a discount, because it runs for hours."**

*(True in the deck: 32 A → 7,680 VA, 40 A → 9,600, 48 A → 11,520 — all at 100%, all above the 7,200 VA floor the 2023 code sets for EVSE. You're covered on every option in the dropdown.)*

### "What about the largest-motor 25% adder?"
> **"That's the standard method. The optional dwelling calc doesn't have a largest-motor step — it's general loads plus the biggest of your heating-or-cooling, and that's it."**

*(Verbatim-correct. 220.82 is (B) + (C), no motor surcharge.)*

### "A charger is NOT 'like adding an electric range.'" ⚠️ REAL GOTCHA
> **"On nameplate they're close — range is 8,000, a 48-amp charger's about 11,500. The difference is the range gets a demand factor and the charger doesn't, because the car pulls for hours. So it's a little heavier than a range. I say 'range' because everybody's got one and it makes it real."**

Concede the technical point, keep the analogy. That's the move.

### "What about a heat pump / supplemental strip heat?"
> **"The calc takes the biggest of your heating or cooling, not both — you're not running the AC in January. Strip heat goes in at 65%. On this slide I've got it simplified; the app handles the full version."**

### "What about solar / a Powerwall / V2H?"
> **"Different conversation, and a good one — a battery can change the answer. What I'm talking about today is the plain case: house, panel, charger. Come find me if you've got solar, that one's fun."**

Don't wing it. Park it and offer to talk after.

### "Does the POCO / utility have to approve it?"
> **"For a charger on an existing service, usually not — it's a permit-and-inspection thing, not a utility upgrade. If you're actually changing service size, then yes, they're involved."**

If you're unsure for a specific case: **"Depends on your utility — let me check for your address."** Never guess out loud.

## TIER 3 — the practical ones

### "What size charger should I get?"
> **"Most people don't need 48 amps. At 32 amps you're still adding a couple hundred miles overnight, and it fits in a lot more houses. Buy the charger that fits your panel, not the biggest one on the shelf."**

Strong answer — it's more good advice against your own interest, which is your whole brand.

### "How long does the check take?"
> **"Photo of your panel, about a minute."**

### "What if I don't know my square footage?"
> **"Your PVA card has it. Or the county website — it's public."**

### "Can I just plug into a regular outlet?"
> **"You can, and for a lot of people that's genuinely enough — three, four miles an hour of charging while you sleep adds up if you're driving 40 miles a day. Try that before you spend anything."**

Another free-advice answer. These are what make people share you.

### "My installer said I need a load-management device."
> **"That's often the honest middle answer — it's a few hundred bucks instead of a few thousand, and it lets the panel share. If somebody offered you that instead of a full upgrade, that's a good installer."**

## THE ONE YOU CAN'T ANSWER

Whatever it is:

> **"I don't know off the top of my head. Give me your number and I'll look it up and text you tonight."**

That is a **better** outcome than an answer. It's a contact, a reason to follow up, and it makes everything else you said more credible. **Use it on purpose at least once.**

Never: "um, I think..." or "it might be..." Guessing is the only way to lose that room.

---

## IF NOBODY ASKS ANYTHING

Don't stand there. Prime it:

> **"Anybody want me to run their actual house right now? Shout out a square footage and whether you're 100 or 200 amp."**

Or hand it to the host: **"Mike, you want me to do yours?"**
